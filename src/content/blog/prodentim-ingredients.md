Before you buy any supplement, the label is the first thing to read. This post breaks down the **ProDentim** ingredient list in plain language, explains what the numbers mean, and gives you a simple checklist for judging any oral probiotic.

## What is inside ProDentim?

Each chewable tablet is listed as containing **3.5 billion CFU** of probiotic bacteria, plus a few supporting ingredients:

- **Lactobacillus paracasei**
- **Bifidobacterium lactis BL-04**
- **Lactobacillus reuteri**
- **Inulin**
- **Malic acid**
- **Tricalcium phosphate**

The three strains are the "active" part. The rest help with texture, taste, and feeding the bacteria.

## What does CFU mean and why does it matter?

CFU stands for **colony-forming units**. It counts how many living bacteria are in each dose. A bigger number is not automatically better. What matters is which strains are used, whether they are alive when you take them, and whether they have been studied for the job.

## Are the ProDentim strains studied for oral health?

Some of them are. *Lactobacillus reuteri* in particular has small studies in people with gum inflammation, with modest improvements in gum health and plaque. The research on the other strains in the mouth is thinner.

Two honest limits to keep in mind:

- **Results are usually small**, and they tend to fade when people stop taking the probiotic.
- **No study has tested ProDentim itself**, as far as we could find. The research is on individual strains.

## Does ProDentim have side effects?

Probiotics are generally well tolerated, and some people notice mild stomach upset such as gas or bloating when they start. If you are pregnant, have a weakened immune system, or take regular medication, check with your doctor or dentist first. Stop and ask for advice if you get any reaction you are unsure about.

## 6 things to check on any oral probiotic label

1. **The strains are named in full.** A label that only says "probiotic blend" tells you nothing. Look for genus, species, and strain.
2. **The CFU count is clear.** Look for the number per serving and whether it is guaranteed through the end of shelf life.
3. **The strains have research behind them.** Search the strain name and see whether any human studies exist for oral health.
4. **The extras are listed.** Sweeteners, fillers, and flavours should all be on the label, especially if you avoid certain ingredients.
5. **The claims are realistic.** Be wary of products that promise to cure gum disease or replace your dentist.
6. **The guarantee is real.** Check the money-back terms and how to claim them before you buy.
