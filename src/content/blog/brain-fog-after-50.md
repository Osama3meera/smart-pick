Your thoughts feel slow. Words will not come. You start a task and lose the thread. If you are over 50, this kind of mental fog can be frustrating and a little scary.

The good news is that brain fog often has everyday causes you can fix. Below we explain what it is, the 7 most common causes, what helps, and where **Phytomem One** fits in.

## What is brain fog?

Brain fog is not a medical diagnosis. It is a general word for feeling mentally cloudy: trouble concentrating, slow thinking, forgetfulness, or feeling "not sharp". It is a symptom, and there can be many reasons behind it.

## What causes brain fog after 50?

Usually it is a mix of factors, and many of them are treatable. Our list below covers the most common ones. Hormone changes, long-term stress, and life changes can add to them.

## 7 common causes of brain fog

1. **Poor sleep.** Short or broken sleep is the most common cause. Your brain clears waste and stores memories while you sleep, so aim for 7 to 8 hours.
2. **Stress and anxiety.** A mind full of worries has less room to focus. Slow breathing, walks, and talking to someone can help.
3. **Low vitamin B12.** B12 problems are common with age and some medicines. A simple blood test can check it, and it is easy to treat.
4. **Thyroid problems.** An underactive thyroid can cause slow thinking, tiredness, and low mood. Again, a blood test finds it.
5. **Medicine side effects.** Some medicines for sleep, allergies, pain, or blood pressure can cloud thinking. Ask your doctor or pharmacist before changing anything.
6. **Dehydration and blood sugar swings.** Too little water, or big highs and lows in blood sugar, can leave you foggy and tired.
7. **Too little movement.** Sitting most of the day lowers blood flow to the brain. A daily walk helps more than most people expect.

## Is brain fog the same as dementia?

No. Brain fog is usually temporary and linked to something fixable, such as sleep, stress, or a vitamin level. Dementia is a progressive condition that gradually affects daily life. If memory problems are getting worse, or are worrying you or your family, please see a doctor.

## What helps brain fog?

Start with the basics:

- Fix your sleep routine first
- Move for 20 to 30 minutes most days
- Eat regular meals with plenty of vegetables, fish, nuts, and olive oil
- Drink water through the day
- Ask your doctor to check B12, thyroid, and blood sugar

Most people notice a difference in a few weeks of steady habits.

## Can supplements help brain fog?

Supplements can fill gaps, but they cannot fix poor sleep or stress. If a blood test shows a deficiency, such as low B12, treating it can make a clear difference. For other supplements, the evidence is mixed, and most formulas have not been tested as a whole.

Make an appointment if brain fog lasts more than a few weeks, keeps getting worse, or comes with confusion, getting lost in familiar places, or changes in mood or behaviour. A sudden change needs urgent medical help.
