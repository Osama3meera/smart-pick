## How does it claim to work?

According to the creators, the track uses "advanced sound patterns" designed to encourage **gamma brainwaves**, the fast brainwave activity linked to focus and processing information.

They connect this to **BDNF** (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), a protein that helps brain cells grow and communicate. It is often described as "fertilizer for the brain", and healthy BDNF levels are linked with learning and memory.

## What does the science say?

Here is the honest part.

- **Gamma brainwaves are real and actively studied.** Researchers have tested sound and light flickering at around 40 Hz to see whether it affects the brain. The results are interesting, but much of the work has been done in animals, and studies in people are still early.
- **BDNF really does matter for memory.** The best-proven way to raise it is regular physical exercise.
- **The Brain Song's own site does not cite specific studies** on the track itself, and it states that it is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

So it is fair to call The Brain Song a low-effort routine to try, not a proven treatment. The 90-day guarantee means you can test it and decide for yourself.

## 6 free habits that support a sharper memory

Whether or not you try The Brain Song, these habits are backed by solid research:

1. **Move every day.** Walking, cycling, or a short workout raises BDNF and improves blood flow to the brain.
2. **Protect your sleep.** Your brain stores memories while you sleep. Aim for 7 to 9 hours.
3. **Use spaced repetition.** Review new information after one day, then three days, then a week. It sticks far better than cramming.
4. **Eat for your brain.** Fish, nuts, berries, leafy greens, and olive oil are linked with better brain health.
5. **Manage stress.** Long-term stress hurts memory. Even five minutes of slow breathing helps.
6. **Keep learning.** A new language, instrument, or skill keeps your brain building new connections.

Listening to a 12-minute track can sit nicely alongside these habits. For example, you could use it as a calm break after a walk or before studying.
