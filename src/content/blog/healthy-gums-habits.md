You spit after brushing and see pink. Your gums feel tender, or they look puffier than they used to. Many people ignore it, but gums are worth looking after: they hold your teeth in place.

Below are the early warning signs, 7 simple habits that protect your gums, and an honest look at where an oral probiotic like **ProDentim** fits in.

## What are the early signs of gum disease?

Gum trouble often starts quietly. Watch for:

- **Bleeding** when you brush or floss
- **Red, swollen, or tender** gums
- **Bad breath** that does not go away
- **Gums that pull back** from your teeth
- **Teeth that feel loose** or a change in how your bite fits together

## Why do gums bleed when you floss?

In most cases, bleeding is a sign of **inflammation** caused by plaque sitting along the gum line. It often happens when you start flossing again after a break. If you keep cleaning gently every day, the bleeding usually settles within a week or two. If it does not, see a dentist.

## Can you reverse early gum problems?

Early gum inflammation, called **gingivitis**, can often be reversed with good daily cleaning and a dental check-up. Once it has progressed to deeper gum disease, you need professional treatment. That is why catching the early signs matters.

## 7 habits that protect your gums

1. **Brush gently along the gum line.** Use a soft brush at a 45 degree angle and short, light strokes. Hard scrubbing can hurt your gums.
2. **Clean between your teeth every day.** Floss or interdental brushes remove the plaque your toothbrush cannot reach.
3. **Use a fluoride toothpaste.** It protects the teeth that your gums hold in place.
4. **Do not smoke.** Smoking is one of the biggest risk factors for gum disease, and it can hide the early signs.
5. **Eat more fruit and vegetables.** Crunchy produce and a balanced diet support healthy gums, and cutting back on sugary snacks reduces plaque.
6. **Manage stress and sleep.** Stress and poor sleep are linked with more gum problems.
7. **Book regular dental cleanings.** A dentist or hygienist can remove hardened plaque that you cannot get off at home.

## Do probiotics help gums?

Some studies suggest that certain oral probiotic strains, such as *Lactobacillus reuteri*, may modestly improve gum inflammation and plaque in people with gingivitis. The research is still limited, the studies are small, and the effects tend to fade when people stop.

So a probiotic is a possible extra, not a replacement for cleaning and dental visits.

## When should you see a dentist?

Book an appointment if you have bleeding that lasts more than two weeks, gums that are pulling away, a loose tooth, pain when chewing, or a bad taste that will not go away. A supplement cannot diagnose these, and early treatment is cheaper and easier.
